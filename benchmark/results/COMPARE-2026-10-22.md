# Cross-engine DEVStone record, 2026-10-22

This record compares pyjevsim `8135542` (main after #41 and #42) with the
previous main `9a2b0d2` and with xdevs.py, PythonPDEVS and the in-repository
reference engine. All runs used `benchmark/run_compare.py` on one machine on
2026-10-22 between 09:16:41 and 09:19:50 +09:00. Both pyjevsim versions ran
byte-identical harness and adapter code. Every cell is the best of three runs
unless stated otherwise. Synthetic CPU work was disabled
(`int_cycles == ext_cycles == 0`), so the numbers measure kernel, scheduling
and routing overhead, not application-level performance.

An independent review re-ran a subset of the cells and judged the results
sound with the caveats listed under [Caveats](#caveats). Read the caveats
before quoting any ratio.

### PythonPDEVS edits

The clone required the same three Python 3 compatibility edits that
`BASELINE.md` documents. Upstream had not fixed any of them at `53cad29`.

1. `src/pypdevs/minimal.py:101`: `from schedulers.schedulerAuto import SchedulerAuto as Scheduler`
   changed to `from pypdevs.schedulers.schedulerAuto import ...`. Without it
   every run raises `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'schedulers'`.
2. `src/pypdevs/minimal.py:218`: `transitioning.iteritems()` changed to
   `transitioning.items()`. Without it every run raises `AttributeError`.
3. `src/pypdevs/schedulers/schedulerHS.py:46`: `self.heap = []` changed to
   `self.heap = [(float('inf'), 1)]` (the scheduler's own infinite-time
   sentinel). The single-seed DEVStone grid works without it because every
   transition happens at t = 0. Multi-event mode and
   `benchmark/run_sparse.py` advance time and raise `IndexError` in
   `SchedulerHS.readFirst` without it. The edit does not change any DEVStone
   transition count.

The PythonPDEVS adapter's `is_available()` only imports
`pypdevs.minimal`, so it reports the engine as available even when edit 1
is missing and every run fails. Check a real run, not only `--list-engines`.

Observations:

- Today's old engine (`9a2b0d2`) reproduces the BASELINE pyjevsim column
  within about ±10 % up to 6 × 6. At larger grids it stays near 675 k
  (0.95 at 8 × 8, 0.80 at 10 × 10), while BASELINE kept rising to 844 k.
- xdevs, PythonPDEVS and reference run 0.64-1.04 of their BASELINE values
  here; on the larger grids this machine and CPython 3.14 are about
  15-35 % slower for those engines.
- pyjevsim matches BASELINE while the other engines are slower. Either the
  BASELINE pyjevsim was an earlier, slower commit than `9a2b0d2`, or the
  Python version affects the engines differently (for example through the

  cannot separate these causes.
- In BASELINE, pyjevsim at HI 10 × 10 was 1.09× xdevs, 0.42× PythonPDEVS and
  0.31× reference. Today pyjevsim new is 2.21× xdevs (inject), 0.83×

  0.75× with 100 seeds. These changes mix engine changes with the

  are not a measure of pyjevsim improvement since May.

## Caveats

1. **Flat and hierarchical models are compared.** The pyjevsim, PythonPDEVS
   and reference adapters build a hand-flattened DEVStone graph (a seeder
   that fans out to every atomic plus the intra-level chains, with no
   coupled models). pyjevsim does not use `StructuralModel`, and the
   PythonPDEVS minimal kernel flattens with `directConnect` inside
   `Simulator(root)`, outside the timed window. xdevs runs the canonical
   hierarchical `xdevs.examples.devstone` with d nested Coupled models, a
   Coordinator per level, and one extra `bench_top` level in multi-event
   mode. The new/xdevs ratio therefore compares pyjevsim's flat kernel with
   xdevs' hierarchical coordinator. With `flatten=True`, xdevs is about 30 %
   faster (HI 10 × 10, 100 seeds: 635 k against 488 k), which lowers
   new/xdevs from about 3.0 to about 2.2. pyjevsim's `StructuralModel` path
   was not benchmarked.
2. **The single-seed xdevs column measures different work.** On HO the
   inject path does fewer transitions (26 against 38 at 4 × 4, 72 against
   152 at 6 × 6). HO has two root inputs; the second `inject()` runs the
   deltint of atomics the first one activated without their output
   function, so their outputs are lost. In addition, every `inject()` runs
   the first wave of external transitions before the `sim_s` timer starts,
   but those transitions are still counted. This inflates LI by about 2-3×
   (LI 6 × 6: 831 k with inject, 294 k with the generator, same 52
   transitions). The xdevs column in `BASELINE.md` has the same flaw. Use
   the 100-seed table or `--xdevs-seed generator` for xdevs. For xdevs,
   `--events 1` (inject) and `--events > 1` (generator plus an extra
   Coupled level) are different mechanisms, so per-transition throughput is
   not like-for-like between the two modes.
3. **Single-seed cells are too short for precise ratios.** They take
   0.02 ms (2 × 2, 4 transitions) to 1.4 ms (xdevs HI 10 × 10), so timer
   resolution and fixed per-call overhead dominate. Ratios such as
   new/reference 0.26 at 2 × 2 are indicative only.
4. **Comparisons with `BASELINE.md` cross machines, Python versions and an
   unknown commit.** The ratio "pyjevsim new = 1.44-1.67× BASELINE
   pyjevsim" and the changes in cross-engine ratios since 2026-05-05 must
   not be read as engine improvement. Why the old engine stops scaling at
   large grids (an earlier BASELINE commit, or a CPython 3.14 type-version
   cap effect) is not established.
5. **Old against new was measured on CPython 3.14.0 only.** The harness,
   adapters and transition counts are identical for both versions. Part of
   the gain comes from #41, which avoids CPython's per-type
   version-reassignment cap; once that cap is reached the interpreter stops
   specialising attribute access for the rest of the process. The ratio may
   be smaller on Python 3.10-3.12. The old engine's speed also depends on
   how many `SystemObject`s the process has already created, so within a
   grid run its early small cells are favoured; this is consistent with
   new/old rising from 1.32 (HI 2 × 2) to 1.96 (HI 10 × 10).
6. **Run-to-run variation is about 3-10 %.** The reviewer's reruns of HI
   10 × 10 with 100 seeds came out 0.7-9.3 % above the reported values
   (pyjevsim new 1.448 M, xdevs 506 k, PythonPDEVS 1.976 M, reference
   1.864 M, pyjevsim old 631 k; new/old 2.29), and single-seed LI 6 × 6
   pyjevsim new came out 11 % lower (695 k). Only the best of N is reported;
   cells were not interleaved, and old and new ran in separate batches a
   few minutes apart under similar load. Differences under about 10 %, such
   as PythonPDEVS against reference in the 100-seed table, are within
   noise.
7. **The timed windows differ slightly.** pyjevsim's `sim_s` includes
   executor creation and the first schedule build, because
   `SysExecutor.simulate()` performs `create_entity` on its first schedule.
   The other adapters run the equivalent setup (`initialize()`,
   `Simulator(root)`) before the timer starts. This costs pyjevsim 4.3 % of
   `sim_s` at single-seed HI 10 × 10 and 0.1 % with 100 seeds.
8. **Garbage collection was left enabled.** pyjevsim allocates a message
   object per output and is about 9 % faster with `gc` disabled (HI
   10 × 10, 100 seeds: 1.428 M against 1.311 M); the other engines do not
   change measurably. This is a real cost of pyjevsim, but it adds variance
   and order dependence.
9. **HO equals HI in three adapters.** The pyjevsim, PythonPDEVS and
   reference adapters build HO with exactly the HI couplings. Their HO rows
   repeat HI and do not exercise HO's two-port structure; only xdevs builds
   the real two-port HO.
10. **Only zero-cost transitions were measured.** With
    `int_cycles == ext_cycles == 0` the results show kernel and routing
    overhead, not application-level performance.
11. **Reproducibility depends on local patches.** PythonPDEVS needs the three
    edits above, and multi-event mode fails on an unpatched upstream checkout
    (edit 3). The multi-event harness was uncommitted at capture time;
    record its commit when it is merged.
